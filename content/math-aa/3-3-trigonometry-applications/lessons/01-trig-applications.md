## Elevation and depression

```keyidea
Both angles are measured from the horizontal. The angle of elevation from A to B equals the angle of depression from B to A (alternate angles).
```

```figure
src: math-aa/_sme/3-3-trigonometry-toolkit/img/fig-61aa725c1c48449a.png
caption: Angles of elevation and depression are both measured from the horizontal.
```

```figure
src: math-aa/_iitian/ib-mathematics-aa-applications-of-trigonometry-study-notes/img/fig-b16248915b8a3fde.png
caption: The angle of elevation looks up from the horizontal; the angle of depression looks down from it.
```

```example
From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 35°. Find the height of the tower.
---
$h = 40\tan35° = 28.0$ m.
```

## Bearings

Bearings are measured **clockwise from north** and written with three figures (e.g. 045°, 230°).

```figure
src: math-aa/_iitian/ib-mathematics-aa-applications-of-trigonometry-study-notes/img/fig-d87face727cc9a3e.png
caption: A true bearing is measured clockwise from north, as a three-figure angle.
```

```figure
src: math-aa/_sme/3-3-trigonometry-toolkit/img/fig-73a09bc6cebeba49.png
caption: Bearings problems: draw a north line at every point, then use angle facts to build the triangle.
```

```example
A ship sails 12 km on a bearing of 060°, then 8 km on a bearing of 150°. Find its distance from the start.
---
The turn between the two legs is 90° (150° − 60°), so the angle at the turning point is 90°. Distance $= \sqrt{12^2 + 8^2} = 14.4$ km.
```

## From words to diagram

Read carefully, sketch, label all given lengths and angles, then decide which triangle and which rule to use. Problems often need two triangles in sequence.
