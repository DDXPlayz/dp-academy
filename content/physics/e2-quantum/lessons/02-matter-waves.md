## de Broglie’s hypothesis

If light waves can behave like particles, perhaps particles can behave like waves. De Broglie proposed that any particle with momentum $p$ has a wavelength

$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}
$$

```example
An electron is accelerated from rest through 150 V. Find its de Broglie wavelength.
---
$p = \sqrt{2meV} = \sqrt{2\times9.11\times10^{-31}\times1.60\times10^{-19}\times150} = 6.6\times10^{-24}$ kg m s⁻¹.

$\lambda = h/p = 6.63\times10^{-34}/6.6\times10^{-24} = 1.0\times10^{-10}$ m — about the spacing of atoms in a crystal.
```

## Evidence: electron diffraction

A beam of electrons fired through a thin film of graphite produces **rings** on a fluorescent screen — a diffraction pattern, just like light through a grating. The crystal’s atoms act as the "slits".

- Increase the accelerating voltage → electrons have more momentum → shorter wavelength → **smaller rings**.
- The measured ring sizes match the de Broglie wavelength.

```keyidea
Diffraction is a wave behaviour. Electrons diffracting through a crystal is direct evidence that matter has wave properties.
```

## Wave–particle duality

Light and matter both show two faces:

| | Wave behaviour | Particle behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Diffraction, interference | Photoelectric effect, Compton scattering |
| Electrons | Electron diffraction | Deflection in fields, single impacts on a screen |

Neither picture is complete on its own. Which behaviour we see depends on the experiment.

## Summary

- Every particle has $\lambda = h/p$.
- Electron diffraction proves matter has wave properties; faster electrons give smaller rings.
- Light and matter both show wave–particle duality.
