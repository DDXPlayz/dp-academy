definitions:
  - term: Resolution
    definition: The smallest change in a quantity that an instrument can detect (e.g. 1 mm for a metre rule).
  - term: Parallax error
    definition: An error in reading a scale caused by viewing it from an angle rather than straight on.
  - term: Zero error
    definition: A systematic error where an instrument does not read zero when it should.
mistakes:
  - Timing one oscillation with a stopwatch. Reaction time dominates — time 10 or 20 and divide.
  - Connecting an ammeter in parallel. Ammeters go in series, voltmeters in parallel.
tips:
  - Measure many and divide — the thickness of 100 sheets, the period of 20 swings — to reduce percentage uncertainty.
  - Use a fiducial (reference) marker at the centre of an oscillation to start and stop timing.

## Choosing the instrument

| Quantity | Typical instrument | Resolution | Good practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | Metre rule / vernier calipers / micrometer | 1 mm / 0.1 mm / 0.01 mm | Read at eye level; check for zero error |
| Mass | Electronic balance | 0.01 g | Tare (zero) the balance first |
| Time | Stopwatch / light gates + data logger | 0.01 s / 0.001 s | Time many cycles; light gates remove reaction time |
| Temperature | Thermometer / temperature probe | 1 °C / 0.1 °C | Stir; wait for a steady reading |
| Current | Ammeter (in series) | 0.01 A | Check the range |
| Potential difference | Voltmeter (in parallel) | 0.01 V | Check the range |
| Angle | Protractor | 1° | Use long lines to extend the angle |
| Light / sound intensity | Light sensor / sound-level meter | — | Shield from stray light/noise |

## Reducing uncertainty

```keyidea
Repeat and average to reduce random error. Measure a large quantity (many oscillations, many layers) to reduce the percentage uncertainty. Check zero errors and calibrate to remove systematic error.
```

```example
A student times 20 oscillations of a pendulum as 32.4 s with a reaction-time uncertainty of ±0.2 s. Find the period and its uncertainty.
---
$T = 32.4/20 = 1.62$ s, and $\Delta T = 0.2/20 = 0.01$ s, so $T = 1.62 \pm 0.01$ s. Timing one swing would have given ±0.2 s on 1.6 s — over 12%.
```

## Safety, ethics and the environment

Every investigation needs a risk assessment:

- **Safety** — hot objects, high voltages, lasers (never look along the beam), radioactive sources (tongs, minimal exposure time, store in lead), falling masses (protect feet, use a sand tray).
- **Ethics** — honest recording of data, including anomalies; acknowledge sources.
- **Environment** — dispose of materials responsibly; minimise energy and waste.
