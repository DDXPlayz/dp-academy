## Proton donors and acceptors

```keyidea
A Brønsted–Lowry acid donates a proton (H⁺); a base accepts one. Every acid–base reaction involves two conjugate pairs.
```

$$
\underset{\text{acid}}{\text{NH}_4^+} + \underset{\text{base}}{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{conj. base}}{\text{NH}_3} + \underset{\text{conj. acid}}{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+}
$$

Conjugate pairs differ by one H⁺: NH₄⁺/NH₃ and H₃O⁺/H₂O.

**Amphiprotic** species can do both: water accepts a proton from HCl but donates one to NH₃. Others: HCO₃⁻, HSO₄⁻, H₂PO₄⁻.

## The pH scale

$$
\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]
$$

- pH < 7 acidic, pH = 7 neutral, pH > 7 alkaline (at 298 K).
- A change of **1** pH unit is a **tenfold** change in [H⁺].

```example
Find the pH of 0.0050 mol dm⁻³ HCl, and [H⁺] in a solution of pH 3.40.
---
HCl is fully ionised: [H⁺] = 0.0050; pH = −log(0.0050) = 2.30.

[H⁺] = 10⁻³·⁴⁰ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³.
```

## The ionic product of water

Water ionises very slightly: $\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^-$.

$$
K_w = [\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-] = 1.00\times10^{-14}\ \text{at 298 K}
$$

```example
Find the pH of 0.020 mol dm⁻³ NaOH at 298 K.
---
[OH⁻] = 0.020; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴/0.020 = 5.0 × 10⁻¹³; pH = 12.30.
```

Ionisation of water is endothermic, so Kw increases with temperature and the pH of pure water falls below 7 when hot — but it is still **neutral** because [H⁺] = [OH⁻].

## Summary

- Acid = proton donor, base = proton acceptor; conjugate pairs differ by H⁺.
- pH = −log[H⁺]; one unit = tenfold.
- Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 298 K.
