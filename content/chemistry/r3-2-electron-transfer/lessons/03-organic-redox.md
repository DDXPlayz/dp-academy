## Oxidising alcohols

Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (Cr₂O₇²⁻/H⁺) oxidises alcohols; it changes from **orange** to **green** (Cr³⁺). [O] represents the oxidising agent.

```keyidea
Primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid. Secondary alcohol → ketone. Tertiary alcohols are not oxidised.
```

| Alcohol | Product | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| primary | aldehyde | distil off as it forms (aldehydes have low bp) |
| primary | carboxylic acid | heat under reflux with excess oxidant |
| secondary | ketone | heat (reflux) |
| tertiary | no reaction | — |

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + [\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
$$

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + [\text{O}] \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}
$$

```example
How would you make (a) propanal and (b) propanoic acid from propan-1-ol?
---
(a) Add the alcohol dropwise to warm acidified dichromate and distil off the propanal immediately, so it is not oxidised further.

(b) Heat under reflux with excess acidified dichromate, then distil off the acid.
```

## Reductions

Reducing agents such as **NaBH₄** or **LiAlH₄** (represented as [H]) reverse the oxidation:

- aldehyde → primary alcohol; ketone → secondary alcohol (NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄);
- carboxylic acid → primary alcohol (LiAlH₄ only; NaBH₄ is too weak).

## Hydrogenation

$$
\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni, 150 °C}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3
$$

Adding hydrogen lowers the **degree of unsaturation**. Partial hydrogenation of vegetable oils makes margarine (and can produce trans fats).

## Summary

- 1° alcohol → aldehyde (distil) → acid (reflux); 2° → ketone; 3° → none. Dichromate turns orange → green.
- Reduction with NaBH₄/LiAlH₄ gives alcohols.
- Hydrogenation with Ni catalyst removes C=C/C≡C.
