## Two kinds of reagent

```keyidea
A nucleophile donates an electron pair to form a bond; an electrophile accepts one. Curly arrows show the movement of an electron pair, from a lone pair or bond to where the new bond forms.
```

**Heterolytic fission** gives ions because one fragment takes both bonding electrons: $\text{C–Br} \rightarrow \text{C}^+ + \text{Br}^-$.

## Nucleophilic substitution

In halogenoalkanes, the C–X bond is polar ($\text{C}^{\delta+}\text{–X}^{\delta-}$). A nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbon and the halide leaves:

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Br}^-
$$

Conditions: warm aqueous NaOH. The halide ion is the **leaving group**.

## Electrophilic addition to alkenes

The C=C double bond is a region of **high electron density** (the π bond), so it attracts electrophiles. The π bond breaks and two new single bonds form.

| Reagent | Product | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| H₂ | alkane | Ni catalyst, ~150 °C |
| Br₂ / Cl₂ | dihalogenoalkane | room temperature |
| HBr / HCl | halogenoalkane | room temperature |
| H₂O | alcohol | steam, H₃PO₄ catalyst |

```example
What forms when ethene reacts with (a) bromine, (b) hydrogen bromide, (c) steam?
---
(a) 1,2-dibromoethane, CH₂BrCH₂Br. (b) bromoethane, CH₃CH₂Br. (c) ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH.
```

**Test for alkenes:** shake with bromine water. Orange → **colourless** shows a C=C double bond. Alkanes do not decolourise it (in the dark).

## Summary

- Nucleophile = electron-pair donor; electrophile = electron-pair acceptor.
- Halogenoalkanes undergo nucleophilic substitution with OH⁻ → alcohols.
- Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition of H₂, X₂, HX and H₂O.
- Bromine water decolourises with alkenes.
