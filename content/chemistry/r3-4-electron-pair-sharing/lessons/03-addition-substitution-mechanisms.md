## Electrophilic addition: HBr + ethene

1. The π electrons of C=C attack the δ+ H of H–Br; the H–Br bond breaks heterolytically (Br⁻ forms).
2. A **carbocation** forms: CH₃CH₂⁺.
3. Br⁻ attacks the carbocation, giving bromoethane.

With **Br₂**, the approaching π electrons **induce a dipole** in Br–Br; the δ+ Br is attacked, giving a carbocation and Br⁻, which then add to give 1,2-dibromoethane.

## Markovnikov’s rule

```keyidea
In the addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the major product comes from the more stable carbocation. Stability: tertiary > secondary > primary, because alkyl groups donate electron density (positive inductive effect).
```

```figure

caption: Markovnikov's rule: hydrogen adds to the carbon that already has more hydrogens, giving the more stable carbocation.
```

```example
Predict the major product of propene + HBr.
---
H⁺ adds to C1 (CH₂ end) to give the secondary carbocation CH₃C⁺HCH₃, which is more stable than the primary CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺. Br⁻ then attacks: the major product is 2-bromopropane.
```

## Electrophilic substitution of benzene

Benzene’s six delocalised π electrons make it electron-rich, but addition would destroy the very stable delocalised ring. Instead it undergoes **substitution**, which keeps the ring intact.

```figure

caption: Electrophilic substitution of benzene: the electrophile attacks to form a carbocation, then loss of H⁺ restores the aromatic ring.
```

```figure
src: chemistry/_iitian/ib-dp-chemistry-r3-4-13-reactions-between-benzene-and-electr/img/fig-aa2ef64f709716d1.png
caption: Nitration of benzene: sulfuric acid generates the nitronium ion NO₂⁺, the electrophile that substitutes onto the ring.
```

**Nitration** (concentrated HNO₃ + concentrated H₂SO₄, ~50 °C):

1. Formation of the electrophile: $\text{HNO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{NO}_2^+ + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + 2\text{HSO}_4^-$
2. NO₂⁺ is attacked by the ring’s π electrons, forming a positively charged intermediate in which delocalisation is partly lost.
3. A C–H bond breaks and H⁺ is lost, restoring the delocalised ring.

Overall: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (nitrobenzene).

## Summary

- Alkene + HX/X₂: π electrons attack the electrophile → carbocation → halide attacks.
- Markovnikov: more stable (more substituted) carbocation → major product.
- Benzene: electrophilic substitution (e.g. nitration with NO₂⁺) keeps the delocalised ring.
