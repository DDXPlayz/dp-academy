## Structure of the table

- **Periods** (rows): the period number is the outer **main energy level** being filled.
- **Groups** (columns 1–18): elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons and similar chemistry.
- **Blocks**: the sublevel holding the highest-energy electron — s (groups 1–2), p (13–18), d (3–12), f (lanthanides and actinides).
- Metals on the left, non-metals on the right, metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te) along the staircase.

## The key idea

```keyidea
Across a period, nuclear charge increases while electrons enter the same main level, so shielding barely changes — outer electrons are pulled closer and held more tightly. Down a group, a new shell is added each period — outer electrons are further away and more shielded, so they are held less tightly.
```

```figure
src: chemistry/_iitian/the-periodic-table-s3-1-3-periodicity-of-properties-ib-dp-ch/img/fig-46cc8ac5ea0a71db.png
caption: Atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group.
```

| Property | Across a period → | Down a group ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | decreases | increases |
| First ionisation energy | generally increases | decreases |
| Electronegativity | increases | decreases |
| Electron affinity (more exothermic) | generally increases | generally decreases |
| Metallic character | decreases | increases |

## Ionic radius

- **Cations** are **smaller** than their atoms: an entire shell is lost and the remaining electrons feel more nuclear charge.
- **Anions** are **larger** than their atoms: extra electrons increase repulsion with the same nuclear charge.
- For **isoelectronic** ions (e.g. N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, all with 10 electrons), radius decreases as nuclear charge increases.

```example
Arrange in order of increasing radius: Mg²⁺, Na⁺, F⁻, O²⁻.
---
All have 10 electrons; more protons pull them in harder: Mg²⁺ (12 p) < Na⁺ (11) < F⁻ (9) < O²⁻ (8).
```

## Group trends

- **Group 1 (alkali metals):** reactivity **increases** down the group — the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more easily lost. They react with water to give hydroxides and hydrogen: $2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$.
- **Group 17 (halogens):** reactivity **decreases** down the group — an extra electron is attracted less strongly by larger atoms. A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its salt: $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KBr} \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + \text{Br}_2$.

## Summary

- Period = outer main level; group = valence electrons; block = sublevel being filled.
- Across: radius ↓, IE ↑, EN ↑. Down: radius ↑, IE ↓, EN ↓.
- Cations smaller, anions larger; isoelectronic ions shrink with more protons.
- Group 1 reactivity increases down; group 17 reactivity decreases down.
