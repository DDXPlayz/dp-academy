xlabel: element (Na → Ar)

- **Mg → Al (dip):** Al’s outer electron is in a **3p** orbital, higher in energy and more shielded than Mg’s 3s electrons.
- **P → S (dip):** in S, the electron is removed from a 3p orbital that contains a **pair**; repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove.

## Transition elements

```keyidea
Transition elements have incomplete d sublevels. This gives them variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, complex ion formation, catalytic activity and magnetic properties.
```

**Variable oxidation states.** The 4s and 3d electrons have similar energies, so successive ionisation energies rise gradually with no sudden jump — the metal can lose different numbers of electrons. All first-row transition elements show +2 (loss of the 4s electrons).

| Element | Common oxidation states |
|---|---|
| Fe | +2, +3 |
| Cu | +1, +2 |
| Mn | +2, +4, +7 |
| Cr | +3, +6 |

**Complex ions.** Ligands such as H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻ and CN⁻ donate lone pairs to the metal ion via coordination bonds, e.g. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. The number of coordination bonds is the **coordination number**.

## Why complexes are coloured

1. In an isolated ion the five d orbitals have equal energy. Ligands approaching the ion **split** them into two sets of different energy.
2. An electron absorbs a photon of visible light and is promoted to the higher set. The energy gap $\Delta E = h\nu$ determines which wavelength is absorbed.
3. The colour we see is the **complementary** colour of the light absorbed.

The size of the splitting depends on the metal, its oxidation state, the ligand and the geometry — so changing any of these changes the colour. Ions with d⁰ (Sc³⁺) or d¹⁰ (Zn²⁺, Cu⁺) configurations cannot undergo d–d transitions, so they are colourless.

```example
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ absorbs light of wavelength around 800 nm (red/orange). What colour does the solution appear?
---
It transmits the complementary colour of red-orange: blue (pale blue solution).
```

## Summary

- IE dips at group 13 (p electron, higher energy) and group 16 (paired p electron repulsion).
- Transition elements: incomplete d sublevel → variable oxidation states, complexes, colour, catalysis, magnetism.
- Colour: ligands split d orbitals; d–d absorption; complementary colour is seen.
