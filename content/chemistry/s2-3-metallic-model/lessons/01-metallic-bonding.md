## The metallic bond

```keyidea
In a metal, atoms release their valence electrons into a "sea" of delocalised electrons that moves through a lattice of positive ions. The metallic bond is the electrostatic attraction between the cations and the delocalised electrons.
```

## Explaining properties

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Electrical conductivity | Delocalised electrons move through the lattice when a p.d. is applied |
| Thermal conductivity | Mobile electrons (and vibrating ions) transfer kinetic energy rapidly |
| Malleable and ductile | Layers of ions can slide over each other; the electron sea keeps holding them together, so the metal changes shape without breaking |
| Lustre | Delocalised electrons absorb and re-emit light |
| High melting point (most) | Strong attractions throughout the lattice |

## Strength of metallic bonding

The bond is stronger when there are:

- **more delocalised electrons** per atom (higher cation charge);
- **smaller cations** (electrons closer to the nuclei).

```example
Explain the melting points: Na 98 °C, Mg 650 °C, Al 660 °C; and Li 181 °C > Na 98 °C > K 63 °C.
---
Across period 3, the ions carry more charge (1+, 2+, 3+), are smaller and release more delocalised electrons, so bonding is stronger. Down group 1, the ions get larger, so the attraction to the electron sea is weaker.
```

## (HL) Transition elements

Transition metals have **delocalised d electrons** as well as s electrons. More electrons in the sea and small ions give them high melting points, high densities and good conductivity compared with s-block metals.

## Summary

- Metallic bond: cations + delocalised electrons.
- Conductivity from mobile electrons; malleability because layers slide while the bonding stays.
- Strength increases with ionic charge and decreases with ionic radius.
