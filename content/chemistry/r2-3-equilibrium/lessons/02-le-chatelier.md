## The principle

```keyidea
When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the position of equilibrium shifts in the direction that partly opposes the disturbance.
```

| Change | Shift | K? |
|---|---|---|
| Add a reactant / remove a product | to the right (products) | unchanged |
| Add a product | to the left | unchanged |
| Increase pressure (gases) | towards fewer moles of gas | unchanged |
| Increase temperature | in the endothermic direction | changes |
| Add a catalyst | no shift (equilibrium reached faster) | unchanged |

## Temperature and K

For an **exothermic** forward reaction, raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left and **decreases K**. For an **endothermic** forward reaction, K **increases** with temperature.

```example
N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict the effect on the yield of ammonia of (a) increasing pressure, (b) increasing temperature, (c) adding an iron catalyst.
---
(a) 4 mol gas → 2 mol gas: higher pressure shifts right — yield increases.

(b) Forward reaction is exothermic: higher temperature shifts left — yield decreases (K decreases).

(c) No change in yield; equilibrium is reached faster.
```

## Compromise conditions in industry

The **Haber process** uses about 450 °C and 200 atm with an iron catalyst:

- a lower temperature would give a higher yield, but the rate would be too slow — 450 °C is a **compromise** between yield and rate;
- higher pressure would give a higher yield, but costs more energy and stronger equipment.

Unreacted gases are recycled, so overall conversion is high.

## Summary

- Disturbance → shift to oppose it.
- Concentration and pressure change the position, not K; temperature changes K.
- Catalysts don’t shift equilibrium.
- Industry chooses compromise conditions balancing yield, rate and cost.
