## Successive ionisation energies

$$
\text{X}(g) \rightarrow \text{X}^+(g) + e^- \qquad \text{(first IE)}
$$

$$
\text{X}^+(g) \rightarrow \text{X}^{2+}(g) + e^- \qquad \text{(second IE)}
$$

Each successive ionisation energy is **larger** than the last: each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion, and the remaining electrons are held more tightly.

## Big jumps reveal shells

```keyidea
A very large jump in successive ionisation energies means the next electron comes from a main energy level closer to the nucleus — less shielded and much more strongly attracted.
```

For sodium there is a huge jump between the 1st and 2nd IE, so sodium has **one** outer electron — group 1. The next jump comes after electron 9, marking the change from n = 2 to n = 1.

```example
An element has successive ionisation energies (kJ mol⁻¹): 738, 1451, 7733, 10 540, 13 630. Which group is it in?
---
The big jump is between the 2nd (1451) and 3rd (7733). Two outer electrons → group 2 (it is magnesium).
```

```question
ie-group
```

## Summary

- Successive IEs always increase.
- A large jump means a move to an inner shell; electrons before the first big jump = number of valence electrons = group (for s and p blocks).
