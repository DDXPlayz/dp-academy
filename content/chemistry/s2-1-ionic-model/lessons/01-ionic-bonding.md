## Forming ions

Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach a stable (often noble-gas) configuration.

| Group | 1 | 2 | 13 | 15 | 16 | 17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical ion | +1 | +2 | +3 | −3 | −2 | −1 |

Transition metals can form more than one ion (Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺; Cu⁺, Cu²⁺).

**Polyatomic ions** to know:

| Ion | Formula | Ion | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| ammonium | NH₄⁺ | nitrate | NO₃⁻ |
| hydroxide | OH⁻ | sulfate | SO₄²⁻ |
| carbonate | CO₃²⁻ | phosphate | PO₄³⁻ |
| hydrogencarbonate | HCO₃⁻ | | |

## The ionic bond and the lattice

```keyidea
An ionic bond is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. It acts in all directions, so ions pack into a giant three-dimensional lattice, each ion surrounded by ions of opposite charge.
```

The formula of an ionic compound is an **empirical formula**: the simplest ratio of ions that gives zero overall charge.

```example
Write the formulas of aluminium oxide, calcium nitrate and ammonium sulfate.
---
Al³⁺ and O²⁻ → Al₂O₃. Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻ → Ca(NO₃)₂. NH₄⁺ and SO₄²⁻ → (NH₄)₂SO₄.
```

## Properties of ionic compounds

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| High melting and boiling points | Many strong electrostatic attractions in the lattice must be overcome |
| Conduct when molten or dissolved, not when solid | Ions must be free to move; in the solid they are fixed |
| Often soluble in water | Polar water molecules are attracted to the ions |
| Brittle | A shift brings like charges together, which repel and split the crystal |

## Lattice enthalpy

The strength of an ionic lattice is measured by its **lattice enthalpy**. It is larger when the ions:

- have **higher charges** (MgO > NaF);
- are **smaller** (NaF > NaCl), so they are closer together.

## Summary

- Metals lose electrons (cations); non-metals gain them (anions).
- Ionic bond = electrostatic attraction between ions; giant lattice; empirical formulas.
- High melting points, conduct only when molten/dissolved, brittle.
- Lattice enthalpy rises with ionic charge and falls with ionic radius.
