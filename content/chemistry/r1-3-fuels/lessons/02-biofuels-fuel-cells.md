## Biofuels

Photosynthesis fixes atmospheric carbon into glucose:

$$
6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2
$$

Fermentation of glucose by yeast (anaerobic, ~37 °C) gives bioethanol:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2
$$

```keyidea
Biofuels release carbon that was captured from the air only recently, so in principle burning them adds little net CO₂. In practice, energy used in farming, fertilisers, processing and transport means they are not fully carbon-neutral.
```

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Renewable | Land use competes with food crops |
| Lower net CO₂ | Deforestation, loss of biodiversity |
| Can use waste (biogas) | Lower specific energy (ethanol vs petrol) |
| | Water and fertiliser use |

## Fuel cells

A fuel cell converts chemical energy directly to electrical energy, with no combustion. Fuel and oxygen are supplied continuously.

**Hydrogen fuel cell (acidic electrolyte):**

- Anode (−): $\text{H}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}^+ + 2e^-$ (oxidation)
- Cathode (+): $\text{O}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^- \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (reduction)
- Overall: $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$

**Methanol fuel cell:** anode $\text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}^+ + 6e^-$.

**Advantages:** high efficiency, water as the only product (hydrogen), quiet. **Disadvantages:** hydrogen is hard to store, mostly produced from fossil fuels, and catalysts (e.g. platinum) are expensive.
