## Combustion

Many substances burn in oxygen: reactive metals ($2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$), non-metals ($\text{S} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{SO}_2$) and organic compounds.

```keyidea
Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon or alcohol gives CO₂ and H₂O. With limited oxygen, incomplete combustion gives toxic carbon monoxide and/or soot (carbon) instead of some CO₂.
```

```example
Write equations for the complete combustion of octane and for its incomplete combustion to carbon monoxide.
---
Complete: $\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + 12\tfrac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 8\text{CO}_2 + 9\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Incomplete: $\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + 8\tfrac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 8\text{CO} + 9\text{H}_2\text{O}$
```

**Carbon monoxide** binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen, reducing the blood’s ability to carry O₂ — it is colourless, odourless and deadly. **Soot** (particulates) causes respiratory problems and blackens surfaces.

## Fossil fuels

| Fuel | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | Abundant, cheap, easy to store | Most CO₂ per unit energy; SO₂ and particulates; mining hazards |
| Crude oil | Easily transported; refined into many fuels and chemicals | Spills; limited reserves; CO₂ |
| Natural gas (CH₄) | Cleanest-burning, highest specific energy, least CO₂ per kJ | Methane leaks (potent greenhouse gas); hard to store |

```figure
src: chemistry/_iitian/energy-from-fuelsr1-3-3-fossil-fuels-ib-dp-chemistry-study-n/img/fig-397e3c6209ba0250.png
caption: Natural gas, petroleum and coal are mainly carbon and hydrogen, with smaller amounts of sulfur, nitrogen and oxygen.
```

Fossil fuels are **non-renewable**: they formed over millions of years from buried plant and animal remains and are being used far faster than they can form. Renewable sources (solar, wind, hydroelectric, biofuels) are replenished on a human timescale.

The **carbon footprint** of an activity is the total amount of greenhouse gases it releases, expressed as the mass of CO₂ that would have the same warming effect.

```example
Which fuel releases the least CO₂ per kJ: methane (ΔH_c = −891 kJ mol⁻¹) or octane, C₈H₁₈ (ΔH_c = −5470 kJ mol⁻¹)?
---
Methane: 1 mol CO₂ per 891 kJ, so 1.12 × 10⁻³ mol CO₂ per kJ.

Octane: 8 mol CO₂ per 5470 kJ, so 1.46 × 10⁻³ mol CO₂ per kJ.

Methane releases less CO₂ for the same energy, because a larger fraction of its energy comes from forming water.
```

## Climate change

Burning fossil fuels releases carbon that was locked away for millions of years. CO₂ (and CH₄, H₂O) absorb infrared radiation emitted by the Earth, enhancing the greenhouse effect and raising the average global temperature, with effects on sea level, weather patterns and ecosystems.

## Summary

- Complete combustion → CO₂ + H₂O; incomplete → CO and/or C.
- CO is toxic; soot harms lungs.
- Coal, oil, gas differ in energy density, CO₂ per kJ and pollution; all contribute to climate change.
