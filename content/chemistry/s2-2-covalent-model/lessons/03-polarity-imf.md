## Bond polarity

**Electronegativity** increases across a period and up a group; fluorine is the most electronegative element. When two atoms with different electronegativities bond, the shared electrons are pulled towards the more electronegative atom, giving partial charges: $\text{H}^{\delta+}\text{–Cl}^{\delta-}$. The bigger the difference, the more polar the bond.

## Molecular polarity

```keyidea
A molecule is polar if it has polar bonds AND its shape does not let the bond dipoles cancel. Symmetrical molecules (CO₂, BF₃, CH₄, CCl₄) are non-polar even with polar bonds.
```

| Molecule | Shape | Polar? |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | linear | No — dipoles cancel |
| H₂O | bent | Yes |
| CCl₄ | tetrahedral | No |
| CHCl₃ | tetrahedral, unsymmetrical | Yes |
| NH₃ | trigonal pyramidal | Yes |

## Intermolecular forces

| Force | Between | Relative strength |
|---|---|---|
| London (dispersion) | all molecules; increases with number of electrons/size | weakest (for similar M) |
| Dipole–induced dipole | a polar and a non-polar molecule | weak |
| Dipole–dipole | polar molecules | stronger |
| Hydrogen bonding | H on N, O, F to a lone pair on N, O, F | strongest |

For comparable molar mass: **London < dipole–dipole < hydrogen bonding**.

```example
Explain the order of boiling points: propane (C₃H₈, −42 °C) < methoxymethane (CH₃OCH₃, −24 °C) < ethanol (C₂H₅OH, 78 °C). All have M ≈ 44–46 g mol⁻¹.
---
Propane is non-polar — London forces only. Methoxymethane is polar — dipole–dipole forces as well. Ethanol has an O–H group — hydrogen bonding, the strongest, so the most energy is needed to separate its molecules.
```

**Solubility:** substances dissolve when solute–solvent attractions are comparable to those they replace. Ethanol dissolves in water (hydrogen bonding); hexane does not.

## Covalent network structures

Some substances are giant covalent **networks** with no separate molecules: diamond, graphite, graphene and silicon dioxide. Melting them breaks strong covalent bonds, so their melting points are very high. Graphite and graphene conduct because each carbon has one delocalised electron; diamond and SiO₂ do not.

## Chromatography

Components of a mixture separate according to their relative attraction to the **stationary phase** (e.g. paper) and the **mobile phase** (solvent). A component strongly attracted to the mobile phase travels further.

$$
R_f = \frac{\text{distance moved by spot}}{\text{distance moved by solvent front}}
$$

## Summary

- Electronegativity difference → bond polarity; shape decides molecular polarity.
- IMFs: London < dipole–dipole < hydrogen bonding (similar M); stronger IMFs → higher boiling point.
- Covalent networks have very high melting points.
- Chromatography separates by attraction to stationary and mobile phases; $R_f$ = spot distance / solvent distance.
