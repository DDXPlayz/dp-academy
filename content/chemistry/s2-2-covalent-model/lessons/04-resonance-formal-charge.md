## Resonance

Some species cannot be described by a single Lewis formula. In the carbonate ion, the double bond could be to any of the three oxygens.

```keyidea
The real structure is a resonance hybrid: the π electrons are delocalised over several atoms, so all the equivalent bonds are identical, with a length and strength between single and double bonds.
```

Examples: CO₃²⁻ (bond order 1⅓), NO₃⁻, O₃ and the carboxylate ion RCOO⁻ (bond order 1½).

**Benzene, C₆H₆:** all six C–C bonds are identical (139 pm, between C–C 154 pm and C=C 134 pm). The six π electrons are delocalised in a ring above and below the plane. Evidence: equal bond lengths; benzene’s enthalpy of hydrogenation is far less exothermic than expected for three C=C bonds; it prefers substitution to addition.

## Expanded octets

Elements in period 3 and below can hold more than eight electrons around them.

| Domains | Lone pairs | Shape | Angles | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0 | trigonal bipyramidal | 90°, 120° | PCl₅ |
| 5 | 1 | seesaw | <90°, <120° | SF₄ |
| 5 | 2 | T-shaped | <90° | ClF₃ |
| 5 | 3 | linear | 180° | XeF₂, I₃⁻ |
| 6 | 0 | octahedral | 90° | SF₆ |
| 6 | 1 | square pyramidal | <90° | BrF₅ |
| 6 | 2 | square planar | 90° | XeF₄ |

## Formal charge

$$
FC = V - \tfrac{1}{2}B - L
$$

($V$ = valence electrons, $B$ = bonding electrons around the atom, $L$ = lone-pair electrons.)

```example
Two Lewis formulas can be drawn for CO₂: O=C=O, or O≡C–O (with three lone pairs on the single-bonded O). Which is preferred?
---
O=C=O: C: 4 − 4 − 0 = 0; each O: 6 − 2 − 4 = 0. All zero.

O≡C–O: triple-bonded O: 6 − 3 − 2 = +1; C: 4 − 4 − 0 = 0; single-bonded O: 6 − 1 − 6 = −1.

The first, with all formal charges zero, is preferred.
```

## Summary

- Resonance: delocalised electrons, identical intermediate bonds (CO₃²⁻, NO₃⁻, benzene).
- Period 3+ atoms can expand their octets: 5 domains trigonal bipyramidal, 6 octahedral.
- Formal charge $= V - \tfrac{1}{2}B - L$; prefer charges near zero.
