## Sharing electrons

```keyidea
A covalent bond forms when two atoms (usually non-metals) share a pair of electrons. The bond is the electrostatic attraction between the shared electrons and both positive nuclei.
```

Atoms often share enough electrons to complete an **octet** (hydrogen: two electrons).

| Bond | Shared pairs | Example | Bond length | Bond strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 1 | C–C | longest | weakest |
| Double | 2 | C=C | shorter | stronger |
| Triple | 3 | C≡C | shortest | strongest |

## Drawing Lewis formulas

1. Count the total **valence electrons** (adjust for charge).
2. Join atoms with single bonds (a line = one shared pair).
3. Complete the octets of the outer atoms with lone pairs.
4. Put remaining electrons on the central atom.
5. If the central atom lacks an octet, turn lone pairs into multiple bonds.

```example
Draw the Lewis formula of carbon dioxide.
---
Valence electrons: 4 + 2(6) = 16. Skeleton O–C–O uses 4; octets on O use 12; carbon then has only 4 electrons. Convert a lone pair on each O into a bond: O=C=O, with two lone pairs on each oxygen.
```

## Coordination bonds

In a **coordination** (dative) bond both shared electrons come from one atom, which must have a lone pair:

```figure
src: chemistry/_iitian/ib-dp-chemistry-s2-2-3-coordination-bonds-study-notes/img/fig-67e35ee894880caa.png
caption: In carbon monoxide one of the three bonds is a coordination bond: both of its electrons come from oxygen.
```

```figure
src: chemistry/_iitian/ib-dp-chemistry-s2-2-3-coordination-bonds-study-notes/img/fig-3e5c66e7ece24a6d.png
caption: Ammonia's lone pair forms a coordination bond to H⁺, giving the ammonium ion.
```

$$
\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{NH}_4^+ \qquad \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+
$$

Carbon monoxide contains one: C≡O, where one of the three pairs came from oxygen. Once formed, a coordination bond is identical to any other covalent bond.

## Exceptions to the octet

Some atoms are stable with fewer than eight: **Be** in BeCl₂ (4 electrons) and **B** in BF₃ (6 electrons). These electron-deficient molecules readily accept a lone pair.

## Summary

- Covalent bond: shared electron pair attracted to two nuclei.
- More shared pairs → shorter, stronger bonds.
- Coordination bond: both electrons from one atom (NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺, CO).
- Be and B can have incomplete octets.
